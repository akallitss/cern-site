ACT 0

Why P2 needs these detectors

The measurement, the spectrometer, and what tracking buys

CORE

P2 at MESA: the weak mixing angle to 0.14 %

Design report: D. Becker et al., Eur. Phys. J. A 54 (2018) 208 — arXiv:1802.04759

The observable

Parity-violating asymmetry in elastic e–p scattering gives the proton's weak charge, and from it sin²θW:

APV−39.94 ppb
target ΔAPV0.56 ppb (1.4 %)
⟨Q²⟩4.5 × 10⁻³ GeV²
beam155 MeV, 150 µA polarised CW
target60 cm LH₂
running time10 000 h
elastic electron rate~0.1 THz

→ sin²θW to 0.14 % relative, sensitive to BSM mass scales of order 50 TeV.

What that demands of a detector

  • Rate. 0.1 THz total elastic rate is the environment. This is why the readout question is not cosmetic.
  • Q² knowledge. An integrating asymmetry measurement only becomes physics if you know Q². That is a tracking job — the forward tracker must deliver ⟨Q²⟩ to 1 %.
  • Robustness. 10 000 h of running means gain stability and spark behaviour are first-class specifications, not afterthoughts.
Two trackers, two technologies: the forward Q² tracker is HV-MAPS; the backward spectrometer — BASKET — is Micromegas. This talk is about BASKET.

Say: "0.14 % on sin²θW is the headline, but the number that shapes my detector is 0.1 THz — and the fact that an integrating measurement needs tracking to know Q² at all."

BASKET: the backward-angle Micromegas spectrometer

Three planes, ≈20 000 channels, inside the 0.6 T solenoid — the configuration measured in July 2026

Why go backward at all

The dominant hadronic uncertainties in extracting sin²θW are nucleon form factors. Backward-angle running measures them directly:

quantityuncertainty improves by
strange electric form factor GEs× 4
strange magnetic GMs× 12
effective axial GA× 10

Design report Sec. 7. So BASKET is not a monitor — it removes the leading systematic on the main result.

The apparatus

  • ≈20 000 Micromegas channels in three detector planes at large backward angles.
  • The three planes reconstruct electron tracks inside the 0.6 T solenoid field.
  • Pad readout: each channel carries a full 2-D position, so there is no strip ambiguity and no micro-TPC reconstruction.
This is the shape of the talk. Three planes, in a row, in the P2 geometry, is exactly what we put on the H4 beam in July 2026 — same plane spacings, same mechanics, same electronics chain.

⚠ Not yet published for BASKET: the spatial-resolution spec, per-plane rate density, timing requirement, and the pad-vs-strip rationale. Those come from collaboration internal notes — hence the clearance question.

Say: "Three planes in a 0.6 T field. Remember that number — three — because the last third of this talk is three of these detectors in a row on a beam."

Making them was the hard part: the mesh buckles during baking

Why there were no working large detectors until this summer

The mechanism

Manufacturing requires a 146 °C bake (ΔT = 121 °C from room temperature) to cure the adhesive. The steel mesh and the PCB do not expand equally:

  • SS316L: 16 × 10⁻⁶ /°C — well known
  • PCB (Shengyi S1000-2M): ≈14 × 10⁻⁶ /°C — not published

The PCB holds the mesh at the pillars and the wall frame, so the mismatch puts the mesh into compression. Pre-tension resists it:

λ = Tpre / (Kmesh · Δα · ΔT)
λ > 1 → survives flat · λ < 1 → buckles at ≈ 25 °C + λ × 121 °C

What the shop floor sees

  • ≥ 10 N/cm: no visible waves after baking
  • < 10 N/cm: waves appear; at 7 N/cm they are narrow
  • At low tension, waves start as early as 60 °C
  • Wave scale ≈ the 3 mm pillar pitch — they form between pillars

Where we got to

An FEM model (FreeCAD + CalculiX, ~100 k nodes) was built, but the bundled CalculiX cannot use a thermal load as the reference load of a buckling step — so the working prediction is the analytical λ above, calibrated on the observation that 10 N/cm is the threshold:

Combined stiffness × CTE mismatch ≈ 8.3 N/m per °C, i.e. ≈1000 N/m of compression at full bake — which is exactly the tension at which waves stop appearing.

The four levers

  1. Raise pre-tension to 12–15 N/cm (margin over threshold) — mechanically harder to hold during gluing
  2. Lower the bake temperature — constrained by the adhesive cure
  3. PCB with CTE closer to 16 × 10⁻⁶ — removes the driver entirely
  4. Tighter pillar pitch — constrained by field uniformity
Still missing, and worth asking this audience: the in-plane CTE of S1000-2M; the effective membrane stiffness E·t of a woven 45 µm / 18 µm SS mesh (vibration method); and the Tg of cured Dynamask.

Outcome: raising the pre-tension above threshold gave us the first batch of flat, working large-area detectors — the four characterised next.

Say: "This is a two-millionths-per-degree CTE mismatch that decides whether you have a detector. Higher pre-tension fixed it — and that is why the detectors in the rest of this talk only exist from this summer." Also a genuine ask: flag the three missing material numbers — someone in the room may have them.

Two front-end readouts, on purpose

DREAM to understand the detector, VMM3a to operate it

DREAM

CLAS12 MVT, NIM A 957 (2020) 163423

  • 64 channels; CSA + shaper + 512-cell analog memory
  • Full waveform per hit — we record 16 samples at 60 ns
  • Peaking time 50–900 ns, 16 settings
  • Externally triggered, dead-timeless to 20 kHz
→ The understanding readout. Waveforms let you fit the clock phase and correct time-walk, so a timing number means something. This is what every efficiency number in this talk is measured with.

VMM3a

ATLAS NSW / RD51 — ⚠ canonical citation to confirm

  • 64 channels; self-triggered, sparse readout
  • Per-hit peak ADC + TDC, no waveform
  • Peaking time 25 / 50 / 100 / 200 ns; gain 0.5–16 mV/fC
  • Per-channel discriminator with a per-chip threshold DAC
→ The operating readout. Self-triggered and sparse is what survives 0.1 THz. But a discriminator throws away the information that lets you diagnose the chamber — which is precisely the story of the last act.
The strategy this justifies: characterise on DREAM where you can see everything, then qualify the VMM against that reference on the same chambers. The July 2026 campaign did exactly this — and it is why we can say what the VMM's limitation is instead of guessing.

Where this goes next: the SALSA MPGD readout ASIC (arXiv:2501.10237) is the longer-term target.

Say: "One readout tells you what the detector is doing. The other is what you can actually afford to run at P2. We deliberately carried both, and that decision is what makes the last slides possible."

SPS, November 2025

Finding the electronics working point

CORE

Nov 2025: the VMM3a shaping optimum

Two pad geometries in the same beam, full gain × peaking-time scan

Method

Each configuration is a run pair, using the VMM's own neighbour triggering to define its noise:

  • signal run: neighbour trigger off → hits where the hardware discriminator fired
  • noise run: neighbour trigger on → sub-threshold hits

Noise width from the MAD (σ = 1.4826 × MAD, robust against the ADC floor); signal from the Landau MPV of the same channels. SNR = MPV / σnoise, computed per VMM and per channel.

Result

  • 100 ns peaking wins on all three geometries.
  • The gain choice sits inside the VMM-to-VMM spread; the plurality vote over all 8 VMMs, with VMM- and channel-level agreeing on 6 of 8, is gain 3.0 mV/fC / 100 ns.
  • The two geometries behave the same — the pad size does not change the electronics optimum.
Keep this in mind for later. This is the SNR optimum. In July 2026 the efficiency optimum came out at gain 4.5 and 200 ns instead — not a contradiction, and the reason is the punchline of Act 3.

Say: "100 nanoseconds, on both geometries. Note that 25 ns was never taken here — we only scanned it the following summer. And hold on to the fact that this is the signal-to-noise optimum, not the efficiency optimum."

Three conventions that change the number

  • Charging-up. det4 rises 74.7 % → 92.3 % over the run and flattens after ~4 h; its rate climbs by a factor 9. The run average, 88.3 %, understates the detector by 4 points. det1 gains 3.4, det2 gains nothing — charging-up is not automatic even within one detector family.
  • Degradation is not charging-up. det3 falls 90.6 → 81.0 % inside two hours at fixed clean HV. Averaging that produces 86 %, which is not a measurement of anything. It is shown, and marked.
  • Masking matters. Before automatic hot-pad masking, 6 pads carried 69 % of all hits on det1 and pinned the centroid — eff_reco read 24.6 %. Eight pads masked, and it reads 93.4 %.

Say: "Same analysis, four detectors, and I will not average a dying detector into a performance plot. det4 is the important one to look at — quoting its run average would have cost me four points of a perfectly good detector."

Bench HV scans: the working point is on a plateau

Gain scan, 16 points. Clean turn-on; plateau from ~400 V at 93.7–94.6 %. The 415 V operating point sits on it.

Drift scan, 12 points. Flat at ~94 % from 565 V. The lowest point is drift = mesh = 415 V, i.e. zero drift field — efficiency collapses to 18 %, exactly where physics says it must. A free null test.

Two things worth saying out loud: the bench reached a plateau, and the bench contains a point where the answer is known in advance and the analysis gets it right. Both are why the beam numbers on the next act are credible.

Say: "The zero-drift-field point is not a failure, it is a calibration of my own analysis — if that point had not collapsed I would not trust the rest of the curve."

CORE

What the bench caught before these detectors ever saw a beam

Framed as methodology: this is the value of characterising first

Two distinct pathologies, each diagnosed to a cause

  • det3 — drift-foil HV decoupling. The dying detector retraces the healthy detector's drift-voltage ladder in peak time, amplitude and efficiency simultaneously; the effective drift voltage walks 400 → 130 → 45 → 0 V. Magboltz rules out gas contamination: every plausible contaminant makes the drift faster, and this got slower.
  • det1 — mesh-contact arcing. Not a monotonic decline but intermittency: has_any goes 64.5 % → 96.1 % → 25.5 % → 10.8 % across four dates. Arrival times in the dead phases are early, death and revival are simultaneous across the plane, and the mesh current spikes at the moment of death.
The methodological point. Neither of these looks like anything in an integrated efficiency number. Both were found because the bench measures efficiency versus time, versus position and versus arrival time at once. Two detectors were kept out of the beam because of it.

Written up in reports/det_lifetime_autopsy_2026-07 and reports/p2_efficiency_intermittency_2026-07.

Say: "I am showing you two detectors that failed, deliberately. The point is not that they failed — it is that the bench told us why, in time, and neither of them wasted beam time."

Bench timing — and one hypothesis killed by simulation

Where the bench got to

  • Time of arrival recomputed from the DREAM waveforms, with a validated fine-timestamp correction and slewing correction.
  • σ ≈ 32 ns on the amplitude plateau; 28.8 ns under best conditions (vertical tracks, amplitude > 600 ADC, single pad, per-channel t₀ equalised).
  • Garfield++/HEED says the physics floor is 3–7 ns — so the 20 ns goal was never physics-limited.
  • The trigger was exonerated by decoding the M3 telescope's own FEU: correlation 0.03, contribution ≲7 ns.
  • A real per-pad structure exists: coherent diagonal bands of ±10–15 ns, verified by splitting each pad's events into independent even/odd halves (r = 0.88).
State plainly: the bench was drift-geometry limited — cosmics arrive at all angles through a 3 mm gap. The beam removes that, and the beam numbers are much better.
Was the per-pad timing structure mechanical? The obvious suspect was mesh sag from the same buckling physics as slide 5. The FEM prediction converted at vd = 28 µm/ns gives 0.13 ns rms against 7.7 ns measured — a factor 57 short, with correlation r = +0.06. It is not mechanical. The conclusion is scale-invariant, so it holds at every pre-tension in the sweep.

Say: "I had a nice story that the mesh flatness was limiting the timing. The simulation says it is off by a factor of 57, so the story is wrong — and it is worth showing because it closes the loop back to the manufacturing slide."

The absolute measurement, in one figure

P2_MID at the working point (mesh 450 / drift 700) — 1 155 012 uRWELL tracks in the fiducial

Method, and the hygiene that makes it defensible

  • Two-point tracks from the front/back uRWELLs (residual spread ≤0.66 mm), projected to each P2 plane through a freely fitted affine frame — which comes out as a proper rotation of −60° with unit singular values and no shear, at every station and every scan point.
  • Denominator hygiene: triggers the probe FEU never recorded are removed from the count, HV-settle and spark-veto windows are removed by time, and tracks must land in the pad fiducial (9 mm).
  • Efficiency = a P2 cluster within 15 mm. The probe-radius scan plateaus by 10 mm with an accidental slope of 7 × 10⁻⁴ per 10 mm → ±0.3 % systematic.

What falls out for free

3.46 mm
residual rms, all three stations = 12 mm/√12
1.05–1.08
pads per cluster

That equals binary pad response with no charge sharing — the resolution is the pad, exactly as designed, and it is the number any Q² statement rests on.

Deliberately mis-setting the channel map moves the efficiency to 60 %. Useful rule for the collaboration: if efficiency reads far below 90 %, suspect the mapping before the detector.

Say: "The residual is 3.4 mm on all three stations, and 12 millimetres over root twelve is 3.46. That is not a fit — it is the pad size. So the detector is doing exactly what a binary pad readout should do."

ε(HV): the bench predicted the beam

NEW

The claim, and it is stronger than we thought

The hardware logbook settles the identity: P2_MID is det1 and P2_OUT is det3, the very chambers the cosmic bench characterised, and neither station was touched during the beam test. So this is the same chamber measured twice — different gas, different probe, different laboratory.

The bench reproduces the shape of the turn-on and predicts the working point. The gas shifts the curve; the shape and the working point do not move.

What both halves agree on

  • A clean, monotonic gain turn-on over ~50 V.
  • The knee in the same place, and the operating point above it.
  • On the beam, no station has reached a plateau by the HV ceiling — and that ceiling is the working point. The bench, at lower gain demand, did plateau.

And on the drift side

  • Beam: 0.157 at zero drift field → 0.896 by 500 V → flattening above ~850 V near 0.974.
  • A separate transparency run pushed to gap 510 V: occupancy falls only 2–3 % with no turnover knee — mesh transparency is still fine past our HV ceiling.

That gap is the discriminator threshold — three independent handles

NEW

The decisive arguments

  1. The threshold DAC alone moves it. The same configuration file 36 minutes apart, with the per-chip threshold lines set or commented out: P2_OUT 0.486 → 0.822. Nothing else changed — same gain, peaking time, HV, gas, beam. Every such pair in the campaign does this.
  2. Amplifier gain moves it, and gain cannot change a detector. 3.0 → 4.5 mV/fC at fixed HV: 0.723 → 0.822.
  3. Pad by pad, efficiency tracks pulse height (r = +0.55 over 69 illuminated pads). A selection effect would run the other way: a channel with a high threshold records only its big pulses, so it would show a high median with a low efficiency.
  4. One curve fits both. A Landau swept past a fixed threshold fits the 11-point mesh scan and is required to pass through the ×1.5 gain point; it returns a gain e-folding of V₀ = 22.4 V — the physical bulk-Micromegas value, and not an input to the fit. The discriminator sits at 0.68 × the most probable signal.

The clincher

The best individual VMM pads already read 0.962, 0.957, 0.954. DREAM measured 0.9604 on that same plane.

A detector cannot be 96 % efficient on some pads and 60 % on others under one uniform beam. A readout with one global threshold per chip and no per-channel trim can — and the VMM's per-channel trim DACs were never used.

Excluded by measurement, not by argument

Tracks outside the active area (all 837 338 are inside) · geometry and channel map on MID/OUT · the coincidence window (3σ→20σ moves it 0.0039) · position matching and clustering (95.7 % of misses have nothing within 15 mm at any time in ±10 µs) · rate, dead time and pile-up (flat against spill time, trigger multiplicity and time since the previous trigger) · the track sample · missing captures (100 % coverage) · charge sharing at pad boundaries · a common-mode DAQ loss (MID and OUT miss almost independently: 52.1 % joint against 50.5 % predicted).

Rate: efficiency is flat, and the fake-match background is not the problem yet

NEW

How the rate axis is built

No pion run was taken, so there is no high-density point. Instead the efficiency is binned inside each sub-run: every bin gives an instantaneous track rate and an efficiency measured under identical conditions — same chamber, same HV, same gas, minutes apart.

Within sub-runs: +0.6 ± 0.4 (MID) and +0.5 ± 0.3 (OUT) points per kHz — consistent with zero up to ~700 Hz of reference tracks, about 2.2 kHz of trigger.

The trap, worth stating

Fitting across sub-runs instead returns +12 points/kHz, which is not a rate effect: P2_MID sits ~4 points higher on 25 Jul than on 27–28 Jul at the same nominal working point, and that offset happens to correlate with rate. Sub-run-average rates are useless here — they are dominated by dead time.

Fake matches

The accidental match probability per 10 mm probe stays between 0.05 and 0.11 % across the whole campaign — the combinatorial background a three-plane coincidence has to reject is negligible at these rates.

Say: "Efficiency does not care about the load we could reach, and the accidental background is a tenth of a percent. The honest caveat is that our lever on rate is a factor two, not a factor twenty — the pion run that would have given that was not taken."

BACKUP

Q: "How do you know your channel mapping is right?"

Four independent validations

  1. Against the M3 telescope on the bench. The within-connector pad ordering was determined from track–hit correlation, not from the connector drawing (r = 0.93 / 0.88), and it came out reversed relative to the naive reading — a real 2-pin gap in the connector.
  2. P2 planes against each other on the beam found a flipped c_5_top ribbon on P2_IN.
  3. The uRWELL reference confirmed it independently.
  4. The VMM readout confirmed it again, through completely different electronics.
Three chains, same answer. And the freely fitted uRWELL→pad transform returns a proper rotation (unit singular values, det = +1) of −60° at every station and every scan point — a wrong map would not do that.

Two sanity checks worth quoting

  • Deliberately mis-setting the map moves the efficiency from 96 % to 60 %. Hence the rule: if efficiency reads far below 90 %, suspect the mapping before the detector.
  • The VMM finds the same dead pad as DREAM — pad 635 (VMM 17 ch 4) at (411, 237) mm, matching the dark square at (410, 237) in the DREAM map. Two electronics chains, same defect, same coordinates.

Where it is still open

P2_IN under VMM readout. Residual 4.7 mm against 3.4 mm elsewhere, with a shoulder at 20–30 mm in the matching distance — a partly wrong channel→pad map on the plane that already had mapping trouble. The uRWELL tracks now make re-deriving that map possible, which is the fix.

Q: "Is the reference telescope good enough?"

The uRWELL reference, characterised

  • Two EIC uRWELL strip planes, ~1 mm pitch, over a 1370 mm lever arm.
  • Front–back residual spread ≤0.66 mm, no rotation between them.
  • Beam divergence understood as a virtual source ~40 m upstream.
  • Rock-stable all campaign at 620/420 V. The fitted frame moves <0.02° over 4.5 h and across all 23 scan points, with no shear (<0.0013).
  • 1.15 M tracks per station in a single sub-run.
Sub-millimetre pointing against a 12 mm pad: the reference contributes nothing to the residual, which is why the measured 3.46 mm is pure pad quantisation.

Systematics, and how they were bounded

  • Probe radius. Efficiency is flat from 8–10 mm out to 40 mm (e.g. 96.05 % at 8 mm → 96.66 % at 40 mm), with an accidental slope of only 7 × 10⁻⁴ per 10 mm. → ±0.3 %.
  • Denominator. Triggers the probe FEU never recorded are removed using the DAQ's own record; HV-settle and spark windows are removed by time.
  • Fiducial. Tracks must land within 9 mm of an instrumented pad; the efficiency is on the active-area plateau.
  • Reference features are visible and identified — a ~5×5 mm hole in the uRWELL appears at the same place in all three stations' maps in the uRWELL frame, which is how we know it belongs to the reference and not to the detectors.

BACKUP

Q: "Why is your bench timing so much worse than your beam timing?"

Because they are different measurements

  • Bench: cosmics arrive at all angles through a 3 mm gap, so the drift path length varies event to event. That is a geometric spread, not a detector limitation. Result: ~32 ns plateau, 28.8 ns best conditions.
  • Beam: muons arrive collimated and normal, so the geometric term largely vanishes. Result: 15.5–22.4 ns per station.

Things ruled out on the bench, by measurement

  • The trigger — decoding the M3 telescope's own FEU gives correlation 0.03 and a ≲7 ns contribution.
  • Mesh planarity — the FEM prediction is a factor 57 too small (r = +0.06). Not mechanical.
  • The gas table — cross-checked against an independent Magboltz run for the same mixture, agreeing to 0.1–0.3 % at every shared field point.
One honest correction to make if it comes up. The bench drift-scan CSV carries an apparent drift velocity 20–30 % above Magboltz. That is not a measurement: it is the measured time spread multiplied by an assumed geometry factor of 0.8, and the data require 0.63 ± 0.03 — stable across a field range over which the Magboltz velocity itself changes by 7 %. Re-fitting it would make it agree by construction. The right thing is to report the measured time spread against the Magboltz prediction, with no free parameter.

Ruled out numerically

Ar/isobutane 95/5 gives ~31 primary clusters/cm, so a 5 mm drift gap sees ~15 clusters:

P(0 clusters) = e−15 ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁷

Three to four orders of magnitude too small to matter, and it survives any plausible gap — even at 3 mm it is 0.02 %.

BACKUP

Q: "Two bugs you found in your own analysis?"

Worth volunteering — it is the reason the VMM numbers changed between August drafts

1. The hot-channel mask was deleting the beam

The rule flagged any channel above 8× its own chip's median occupancy. On a chip whose threshold has been raised, the noise disappears, the median collapses, and the illuminated pads stand 30–50× above it — so they were masked.

  • On P2_MID this removed 64 630 hits arriving exactly at the +115 ns trigger latency. The pads it killed measure 61 % with the mask off and 1 % with it on. VMM 9 was not dead; it was masked.
  • Flagging on out-of-time occupancy does not fix it either: the beam delivers particles at tens of kHz while the matched trigger runs at ~1.4 kHz, so most of a beam pad's hits are out of time with a matched trigger.
  • New rule: an absolute rate. A 12×12 mm pad in this beam takes a few kHz; the pathological channels sit at 10⁵ Hz. Two decades of clear air between them.

P2_OUT — the headline station — is untouched, so the headline number stands.

2. Capture spans started at t = 0

A few hits per capture carry srs_timestamp = 0. Taking each capture's span as (min, max) of its hit times stretched every capture back to zero. Unioned, that made the "was the VMM recording?" cut accept everything and made the live time come out 25× too long. Both now use quantiles.

Why volunteer this. It is a good answer to "how much should I trust these numbers": the failure mode of a masking rule is that it deletes signal, and the way it was caught was that the deleted hits sat exactly at the known trigger latency. That is the kind of cross-check the uRWELL reference makes possible.

Also worth knowing

  • The SRS tick is 22.5 ns, not 25 — which is why VMM σ is pinned there.
  • The trigger fan-out to the VMM only exists from 30 July (run_31), so earlier VMM runs have no matched trigger.
  • DREAM and VMM DAQs number runs independently and drift apart after run_58 — VMM data must be labelled from its own config, never from the DREAM one.

BACKUP

Reference: the det3 autopsy in one figure

The argument

det3 died over ~12 hours at fixed, clean HV with zero sparks (76.6 → 68.7 → 42.9 → 0.5 % over 10 h). Two hypotheses: gas, or a drift-foil HV problem.

The test that separated them

  • As it died, det3 retraced the healthy detector's drift-voltage ladder — in peak time, amplitude and efficiency simultaneously. The effective drift voltage walks 400 → 130 → 45 → 0 V.
  • Magboltz kills the gas hypothesis: every plausible contaminant makes the drift faster. This got slower.
  • The failure front moves patchwise from the inlet corner — consistent with metallisation progressively decoupling from the HV contact.
Verdict: drift-foil HV decoupling. Distinct from det1, whose signature is intermittency, early arrival times in the dead phases, simultaneous death and revival across the plane, and mesh-current spikes at the moment of death — mesh-contact arcing.

Two different diseases, separated by measurement, both diagnosed before either detector reached a beam. Proposed next step: an induced-pulse lab test.
