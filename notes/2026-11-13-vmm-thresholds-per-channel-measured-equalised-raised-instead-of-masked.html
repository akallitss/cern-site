VMM thresholds per channel: measured, equalised, raised instead of masked

The 5-bit trim of every VMM3a channel now works on the P2 lab stand: how much it moves a threshold, how we read it back, what it does to noisy channels, and the commands

Detector R&D 2026-11-13 vmm,daq,p2,threshold,trim,noise,how-to

Until now the P2 lab stand had one threshold per VMM chip, and a channel that was too noisy at that threshold was switched off (masked). Each VMM3a channel also has a small individual correction, the trim, which had never been used with our slow control. During the night of 12 to 13 November 2026 it was made to work, measured on the chips, and used to replace masks. This page gives the numbers and the commands.

Lab stand at Saclay, 10 hybrids, 20 VMM3a, gain 4.5 mV/fC, peaking time 200 ns. Low voltage on, high voltage off: everything here is electronic noise and test pulses, no particles. For where the programs live, see the code map. Chip number = 2 × hybrid + vmm.

Results in short
  • The trim lowers one channel's threshold by about 1.1 mV per step, 31 steps, so about 33 mV in total. That is 4 % of the range of the chip threshold, but twice the distance between threshold and baseline we work at.
  • We can now read back the baseline and the threshold of every channel, in mV, in 9 minutes for all 1280 channels.
  • Equalising brings the spread between the channels of a chip from 3.8 to 0.7 mV rms.
  • Noisy channels are raised instead of masked: one file now runs the 18 detector chips with 1 masked channel instead of 276.
  • 114 channels on three chips were blind because of a configuration effect. One line per channel brings them back.

1. What a trim is

A channel records a hit when its signal rises above the threshold. On a VMM3a:

threshold of a channel = threshold of its chip (sdt, 0…1023) − trim of the channel (0…31) × about 1.1 mV

The trim can only lower a channel. To be able to raise single channels, all channels of a chip first get a trim with some room (for example 20) and the chip threshold is raised to compensate. Nothing has moved, but now a noisy channel can be given a smaller trim, which raises it alone while its neighbours stay where they are.

How the reading back works

The registers of a VMM cannot be read. But the chip can put one internal voltage on an analog pin, and a small ADC on each hybrid measures it. Selecting a channel gives its baseline; one more bit gives its threshold after trimming. The slow-control library on the DAQ machine contains this measurement but offers no command for it, so we wrote a small program of our own that uses the library. Nothing of the installed slow control was changed.

Two traps found on the way. With the slow-control version installed on 5 November 2026 the five trim bits reach the chip in reverse order (writing 1 acts as 16), so trim lines must not be typed by hand: our tool writes the right value and notes the real trim in a comment. And a measurement leaves the chips in a special state: press Configure chip before taking data (the tools do it for you).

An automatic tuner then raises only the channels that need it, a few steps at a time, until no channel is above 50 Hz. For all 18 detector chips it needed 6 captures (8 minutes) and raised 135 channels: 87 by 2 steps, 39 by 4, 8 by 6 and one by 8.

4. How much lower can the chips go?

Less than one might hope, and that is a result in itself. The chip thresholds were lowered four times by 3 mV, tuning again each time. The tuner always ends with a clean capture, but at the last step it has raised four fifths of the channels back to where they were:

Once the channels of a chip are equalised they reach the noise almost together, about 3 mV below our previous lowest point. So what the trim buys is not a much lower threshold. It is a detector with no masked channels, each channel sitting just above its own noise.

5. The blind channel blocks on chips 4, 7 and 9

The first map showed something unexpected. On three chips a block of neighbouring channels (28, 41 and 45 channels) had its baseline 30 to 270 mV above the threshold instead of 15 to 20 mV below it. A channel in that state cannot see a normal signal.

  • In an earlier cosmic run exactly these channels count 0.2 Hz while their neighbours count 1 to 2 Hz.
  • With the internal test pulser, for small and medium pulses, normal channels see 100 % and these channels 0 %.
  • The channels we had masked by hand as "bursting" are those at the edge of the blocks.
  • It is not broken hardware. The shift grows with the gain and disappears when the chip's leakage setting is changed. Something on the input of those channels draws a small constant current; why is not understood yet.

The cure that worked is one line per channel, the leakage bit sl 1. With it the same channels see 100 % of the test pulses and look like their neighbours. One single channel (chip 16, channel 33) is not cured and stays masked.

This was checked with test pulses and high voltage off. How these channels behave with real signals is the first thing to look at in the next cosmic run.

7. The commands

On the DAQ machine, in ~/trim_work/vmm_monitor_tool/ (also in the vmm_monitor_tool folder of the DAQ repository). FILE.txt stands for an exception file in /local/p2/p2testbench/TestBenchCERN/config_ext/. None of these commands changes an existing file: they write a new one, given with --out.

See what a file sets

python3 vmm_trim.py summary FILE.txt
python3 vmm_trim.py trims FILE.txt --chips 7

The first prints one line per chip: chip threshold, gain, peaking time, number of masked channels, range of the trims. The second prints the trim of each of the 64 channels of chip 7.

Raise one or more channels instead of masking them

python3 vmm_trim.py raise FILE.txt --out NEWFILE.txt --chip 7 --channels 12,13 --steps 4 --unmask
  • --chip 7 the chip; --channels 12,13 the channels (a range is written 10-15).
  • --steps 4 four trim steps up, about 4.4 mV.
  • --unmask also removes the mask of these channels.
  • The command refuses if a channel has fewer than 4 steps left. With --clip it goes as far as it can.

Then select NEWFILE.txt in the web page and press Configure chip.

Lower or raise a whole chip

python3 vmm_trim.py sdt FILE.txt --out NEWFILE.txt --chips 2-19 --by -4

--by -4 lowers the chip threshold by 4 counts, about 3 mV. Chips 2 to 19 are the detector chips.

Measure all channels (9 minutes)

python3 vmm_monitor_scan.py map --unmask --bin ~/trim_work/build/vmm_monitor \
    --ext /local/p2/p2testbench/TestBenchCERN/config_ext/FILE.txt --out maps/FILE.json
  • --ext the file that describes the state to measure, with its full path.
  • --unmask lifts the masks during the measurement; a masked channel has no baseline to read.
  • --bin where our measurement program is.
  • It prints a table per chip and, at the end, configures the chips again with the file selected in the web page.

Let the tuner find which channels to raise (high voltage off)

python3 trim_tune.py --ext FILE.txt --tag mytest --k -4 --chips 2-19 --steps 2
  • --k -4 first lowers the chip thresholds by 4 counts (0 keeps them).
  • --steps 2 a channel above the limit goes up 2 trim steps per round.
  • --ch-limit-hz 50 the limit; 50 Hz if not given.
  • --tag a short name that ends up in the names of the files it writes.
  • It takes 60-second captures itself. It starts no run and does not touch the high voltage.

8. What is not done

  • Nothing here was tested with high voltage on or with particles.
  • The existing adaptive threshold scan has a new option that raises a noisy channel before masking it. It passes its tests but has not been run on the stand yet.
  • The DAQ web page has no panel for any of this yet; everything is done from the terminal. The files are ordinary exception files, so the drop-down list, the copy into the run folder and the scans work as before.
  • Our measurement program has to be rebuilt after every new version of the slow control.
  • The origin of the constant current on the three blind blocks is open.

Glossary

Baseline
The voltage at the output of a channel's amplifier when there is no signal.
Exception file
The text file listing the chip settings that differ from the defaults; chosen in the DAQ web page.
Mask
Switching a channel off so that it records nothing.
rms
Root mean square: here, the typical size of the differences between channels.
sdt
The threshold setting of a whole chip, a number from 0 to 1023; one count is about 0.77 mV.
sl
A one-bit setting of each channel that switches off the chip's leakage-current compensation for that channel.
Test pulser
A circuit inside the chip that injects a known charge into chosen channels, to test them without a detector signal.
Threshold
The signal height above which a channel records a hit.
Trim
The per-channel correction to the threshold, 0 to 31; each step lowers the channel by about 1.1 mV.
