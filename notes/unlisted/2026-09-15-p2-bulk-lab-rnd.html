The detector we had to build

Four working chambers (det1 – det4) were needed for the cosmic bench and the SPS H4 beam test in July.

2

Why it was hard

  • A large, flat mesh. ~1700 cm² of mesh must stay flat through lamination, development and baking. Too little tension gives waves; too much and the mesh or the frame gives way.
  • Baking makes waves. The stainless-steel mesh expands a little more than the FR4 board when heated. Where the mesh is held by the pillars and the wall, it is squeezed, and it buckles unless the pre-tension is high enough.
  • Cleaning out the Dynamask. The unexposed resist has to leave through the mesh over the whole fan. Not enough development leaves resist behind; too much detaches the pillars from the mesh.
  • Frames and glue. The frame has to hold the mesh at ~12 N/cm through every step. Frame material and cleaning turned out to matter a lot.
  • The lab itself. De-ionised water and nitrogen quality directly affect the result.

Timeline of all attempts

  • May: 7 dummy bulks in 4 weeks, all failed. Mesh tension was raised step by step, from 3–4 to 10 N/cm.
  • June: frames and glue were the problem. First good dummy bulk on 8 June; det1 two days later.
  • Late June – July: with the steel frames, 4 of the 5 real boards became working chambers at ~12 N/cm.

What we learned: the recipe that works

Process
boardno brushing before lamination
meshASADA 45/19 µm, ~12 N/cm (measured before cutting)
framesteel, not stainless steel; cleaned with soap + hot water + isopropanol / acetone
designevacuation line to help clear the Dynamask during development and rinsing
exposurewall 10–20 min, pillars 20 min
development0.3 → 0.6 → 1.8 (→ 1.8). More than this risks detaching the pillars from the mesh
bake+10 °C / 10 min to 146 °C, 1 h at 146 °C

Things to watch in the lab

  • Check what arrives: the "new" frames were stainless steel. Check the material on every order.
  • De-ionised water: the water was not being filtered. Change the filters on time and check the bottles are full.
  • Nitrogen purity in the bulk lab: keep it checked in the same way.
  • Clean every new frame: two meshes came off frames that were not cleaned properly.
  • HV contacts and drift frame: every problem after the bulk was here. They need a robust, reproducible design.
14

Next steps (to discuss)

  • New insulation masks. Zoned masks where the pillar diameter and pitch change from one pair of connectors to the next, every zone within a 3 % dead-area budget:
    • V3A: same dead area everywhere, pillar Ø 0.35 → 0.80 mm
    • V3B: Ø 0.50 mm everywhere, dead area 2.95 → 0.75 % (how sparse can the support be?)
    • V3C: three dead-area levels × three pillar diameters
  • Open questions for the masks: smallest pillar we can make reliably in 150 µm of Dynamask, and the real lamination growth of the pillars.
  • A reproducible HV contact for the mesh and the drift electrode; a gas-tight drift frame.
  • Re-bulk the 5th board.
