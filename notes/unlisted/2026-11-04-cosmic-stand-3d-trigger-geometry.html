Cosmic stand in 3D: what the trigger geometry alone predicts

Lab cosmic stand at Saclay, scintillator-referenced wk2 scan (runs 181–208). Question: is the statistics pattern of the efficiency maps (kernel radius in maps_K+0.png) a detector effect or the geometry of the trigger with respect to the cosmic-muon distribution? Written 4 Nov 2026.

Detector R&D 2026-11-04 VMM,cosmics,P2,lab,scintillator,acceptance,Monte Carlo,3D

Short answer. For det3 and det2 the statistics pattern is geometry. For det1 it is not, and the cause is in the other two chambers, not in det1.

  • The probe's efficiency never enters. A tag for probe P needs the trigger and a clean cluster in each of the two other chambers. So the bottom row of the maps (kernel radius = tag density) cannot show the probe's own efficiency.
  • det3 and det2: geometry. With every chamber 100 % efficient, the simulation reproduces the measured pattern: densest in the middle of the read-out band, thinning towards its ends and long edges, empty in the corners (section 4a). A muon predicted near an end of the band in one chamber usually misses the band in one of the other two. The paddles are bigger than the band, so the trigger only shifts this a little. The fit puts the bottom paddle about 71 mm lower-left of the top pair along the band.
  • det1: not geometry. Geometry predicts det1 looks like the other two, with the dense region upper-right. The data have it lower-left, with the upper-right end almost empty. Since det1's own response doesn't enter, this comes from how det3 and det2 tag or position the track.
    • The tag-chamber efficiencies don't explain it. Every VMM runs the whole length of the band, so no single weak chip can make a gradient along the band.
    • The likeliest cause is det3's measured position, which enters det1's prediction with weight −1 (det1 = 2·det2 − det3). This is still to check (section 5).

  • Not recorded anywhere: where the paddles sit in x–y. You said they are roughly aligned with the read-out connectors. I started there and fitted both paddle centres and angles to the measured tag positions (section 3).

3. The simulation

  • Muons: I(θ) ∝ cos²θ, with 1 /cm²/min through a horizontal plane (the usual sea-level number). 4×107 muons are thrown uniformly on z = 0 over 1.5×1.4 m and propagated as straight lines.
  • Tag for probe P (as scint_efficiency.py): the trigger fires, and the muon crosses a read-out pad in each of the two other chambers. Each chamber is 100 % efficient. The predicted point is the line through the two measured points, smeared by 3.4 mm (pad pitch / √12) to stand in for the cluster centroid.
  • Fit of the paddle positions: a binned Poisson shape likelihood of the tag points on each probe's read-out footprint, all three probes together, with the MC normalised to the data per probe. It starts from a grid over the top and bottom displacement along the band, then Nelder–Mead.
  • Two models:
    • (a) geometry only;
    • (b) geometry × the measured local efficiency of the two tag chambers, taken from their own probe maps (hit within 30 mm, 200 nearest tags). This shows how much of the tag pattern their inefficiency can explain.

Fit results:

  • Bottom paddle relative to the top pair: (-73, -9) mm with geometry only, and (-72, -12) mm with model (b). Along the band that is 71 and 72 mm toward the lower-left; across the band, +18 mm.
  • Selected muons are therefore tilted by about 3° along the band on average.
  • The statistical errors from the likelihood curvature are 1–2 mm and are meaningless here. The real uncertainty is systematic. Fitting each probe's along-band distribution alone gives a bottom-paddle offset of −150 to −200 mm for det2, around −50 mm for det1, and a weak preference near −250 mm for det3. The det1 residual pulls the combined fit.
  • Paddles centred on the band with no offset is clearly worse: Δ(−ln L) = 1556.

Trigger rate: the paddle telescope alone would trigger at 0.90 Hz with 1 /cm²/min. The measured rate is 0.57 Hz after the spark/trip veto, a ratio of 0.64.

That is plausible: the flux in the building is below the open-sky value, and the paddles and discriminators are not fully efficient.

Coverage: because the paddles are larger than the read-out band, only 0.44 of the triggers cross all three read-out areas, even with perfect chambers. In the data 0.14 of the triggers have a clean cluster in all three chambers.

The "clean-cluster efficiency" is the fraction of triggered muons that cross a chamber's read-out area and leave a clean cluster there (1–6 pads within 15 mm, the tag condition). It is lower than the operating efficiency (det3 0.73, det2 0.67, det1 0.80 at K = 0) because it also needs the cluster to be clean, and because the trigger includes some non-muon or non-crossing coincidences. That is why the measured tag count is about a third of the geometric expectation.

5. What I think the det1 residual is, and how to check

Each VMM reads a strip that runs the whole length of the band; VMM 2 to 7 sit side by side across it. So no single weak or noisy chip, such as det2 VMM 9 or det3 VMM 4/7, can make a gradient along the band. That is also why model (b) changes almost nothing.

The pattern instead follows how much each chamber's measured position enters the prediction:

  • det3's tags come from det2 and det1 and look perfect;
  • det2's prediction takes det3 with weight ½ and shows a mild residual;
  • det1's prediction takes det3 with weight −1 (det1 = 2·det2 − det3) and shows the strongest one.

The common factor is det3's measured position. Two things could cause it:

  • a local position error in det3 along the band, for example in the connectors where the P2_IN order corrections were applied (c3 top and c5 top set to "lin");
  • a z of det3 or det2 that differs from the assumed 320 / 630 mm. This is less likely: it stretches the extrapolation rather than shifting it.

If it is a position error, the det1 efficiency map (top row of the maps) is affected too. A mis-predicted tag counts as a det1 miss, so det1's efficiency in its upper-right is the least trustworthy number on the page.

Checks, cheapest first:

  1. Measure the real chamber heights on the stand.
  2. Plot the det1 probe residual (prediction minus nearest hit) against the along-band position. A det3 position error shows up as a residual shift that changes along the band.
  3. Repeat the half-sample order scan for P2_IN c3 and c5 with the scintillator-confirmed tracks.
  4. Note down where the paddles actually sit on the stand. That would turn the fitted offset into a test.

6. Caveats

  • The paddle positions are fitted, not measured. Their along-band offset is the least constrained number (−50 to −200 mm depending on the probe).
  • No muon energy spectrum or multiple scattering: muons are straight lines. Showers and accidental coincidences are not simulated.
  • A "clean cluster" is a single 100 % efficient hit. Cluster size, noise clusters and masked pads are not modelled. They only change the absolute numbers and the tag-chamber factors, not the geometric shape.
  • The data tails far outside the read-out area (a few % of the tags, mis-predicted) are not in the fit.
