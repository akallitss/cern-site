What you are looking at

The board is the bottom straight edge between x = 302 and 378 mm, as measured from the V1 films and the Apr26 PCB: insulation frame (blue) out to the board edge, the 2.66 mm Dynamask rim wall (green), the 0.57 mm M1 line under its inner border, copper pads, and the 0.8 mm pillars on their real 4 mm lattice. The two Ø3.5 mm screw holes at x = 318.3 and 354.8 mm (y = −7.5) are the ones the tools are pinned to. The PCB core is 0.2 mm, so the board is shown on a flat table.

A · sliding shoe, your way

  • Two runners on the wall's borders (0.35 mm wide, PTFE-faced), one on each edge of the wall. The inner runner is the dam: glue cannot pass under it towards the pillars.
  • The glue cavity between them is fed by the syringe. At the back, a lip 1.0 mm wide sits at the laid-height above the wall and leaves a line of that height.
  • Two overflow channels, one along the inside of each runner, between runner and lip. Glue pushed sideways goes up into them instead of under the runners. They are the inner groove that collects what overflows, so the glue that stays is inside the wall.
  • A fence arm rides against a printed guide bar pinned in two screw holes, which keeps the runners on the 2.66 mm wall. A straight bar for each straight side, an arc bar for each arc.
  • Print the body. Make the runners and lip from shim or PTFE strip glued in: 0.35 mm features are below what FDM holds.

B · film stencil

  • A 50–100 µm PET or Kapton film with a 0.7 mm slot over the middle of the wall, pinned in the same screw holes. It rests on the wall and on the outer pillar tops.
  • The squeegee fills the slot. Lifting the film leaves a line as tall as the film plus the depth of the groove under it (t₁): the slider shows how that adds volume.

The mesh step

In both options the glue is spread by the mesh, as you said. The mesh lands on the highest points: the rails (if t₁ > 0) and the pillars. The glue has to fill the groove up to the mesh and wet it (~40 µm assumed), so the width it spreads to is volume ÷ (t₁ + 40 µm). That is the "after the mesh" number. It must stay under the 2.11 mm between the rails; above 2.66 mm it leaves the wall. A deep groove (large t₁) helps contain the glue, but it also needs more glue before the glue touches the mesh at all.

Still open: the real t₁ (a profile across a developed wall), the glue and its viscosity, and the mesh thickness after stretching. Once these are known, the shoe or the stencil can be drawn for the workshop.
