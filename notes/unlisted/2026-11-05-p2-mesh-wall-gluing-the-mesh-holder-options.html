P2 mesh wall: gluing the mesh, holder options

The rim wall measured on the boards we have (V1 films + Apr26 PCB), how much glue it can take, and three ways to lay the glue line without tape or Teflon on the active side.

Detector R&D 2026-11-05 options to choose from — nothing built yet

The process here is not the usual bulk: the pillars and the rim wall are grown first (insulation film through M1, then Dynamask through M2, about 200 µm), and the mesh is glued afterwards onto the top of the wall. The difficulty is keeping the glue on the wall: tape works on the outside, but on the active side tape or Teflon is messy to remove.

  • Glue path: 1684 mm all round.
  • No room beside the wall on the active side. The first pillar row is 0 to 3 mm from the wall (one pillar on the outer arc, θ ≈ 38°, touches it). Any tool has to stay on or above the wall, never dip down next to it.
  • Alignment. In the files both films sit on the copper crosses to within 6 µm. By hand, each mask cross leaves ±0.45 mm before the copper line touches its outline, so a shifted film moves the wall towards the pads, which are only ~0.5 mm away. The width of the wall itself does not change: M2 alone sets both of its edges.

2. How much glue

The glue does not stay as laid. When the mesh comes down it squeezes the bead flat and the glue fills the mesh openings. What has to be controlled is the volume per millimetre of wall:

laid width × laid height = final width × final height

Aim for a final strip about 1.8 mm wide (inside the groove, clear of both rails) and about 40 µm thick (roughly the thickness of the ASADA 45/19 mesh, an estimate). That is ≈ 0.07 mm³ per mm, or ≈ 0.12 mL for the whole detector.

A 200 µm layer over the full wall width carries about five times too much glue: it is what overflows today. At 200 µm the line must be only ~0.4 mm wide; a wider line has to be thinner.

3. Three ways to lay the line

All three keep the glue on top of the wall, need nothing stuck to the active side, and can be positioned from the board's own screw holes (about 12–15 mm outside the wall).

A · your idea, made precise

Sliding shoe

  • Two PTFE-faced runners ride on the wall; the slot between them lays a 0.7–1.0 mm line.
  • The inner runner is the dam on the active side: nothing to peel off afterwards.
  • Kept on line by a fence on pins in the screw holes: one straight guide per side, one arc guide per arc.
  • Line height set by a feeler-gauge shim (e.g. 100 µm), not by the printer.

− glue flow depends on push speed and pressure; corners need care.

B · recommended first try

Film stencil + squeegee

  • A PET or Kapton film (50–100 µm) with a 0.7–1.4 mm slot along the wall centre, cut from the M2 Gerber.
  • Line height = film thickness, exactly; no 3D-print tolerance involved.
  • Aligned like M1 and M2: same crosses on the film, or pins in the screw holes. The slot has ±0.4 mm of room in the groove.
  • Lift it straight up and the active side is clean: the film is the protection, in one piece.

− lies on the outer pillar tops (light film, no pressure); needs a laser cutter, or 2–4 segments.

C · cheapest, if the rails are real

Fill the groove level

  • The two rails are a stencil already built into the wall: a flat blade riding on them leaves glue only in the groove.
  • The volume comes out right on its own if the groove is ~30–40 µm deep (2.1 mm × 35 µm ≈ 0.07 mm³/mm).
  • No tool to make beyond a straight PTFE-faced blade.

− works only if the rails are actually there; measure first.

4. To settle before making anything

  1. Profile of a finished wall. Run a profilometer or dial gauge across the wall of a developed board (the debulked one would do). This shows whether the rails exist and how high they are (t₁), and decides between B and C. It also shows whether the mesh sits on the rails above the pillars.
  2. The glue: which glue, its viscosity and pot life. A stencil and a squeegee want a paste; a syringe shoe takes a runnier glue.
  3. The mesh thickness after stretching (≈ 40 µm assumed above). It sets the final glue thickness, and with it the volume.

Heights do not come from the Gerbers: the 200 µm is the Dynamask height used in the lab, and t₁ is unknown.
